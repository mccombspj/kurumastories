Toyota Carina 1600ST (1971)

Publication: Car Graphic
Format: Road Test
Date: January 1971
Author: “C/G Test Group” (uncredited)

Road testing the Toyota Carina 1600ST

The Toyota Carina (named after the constellation Carina) was first shown to the public at the Tokyo Motor Show in the fall of 1970, and went on sale on December 1. It is a semi-fastback five-seat sedan closer in exterior dimensions to the Corona 1500/1700 than to the Corolla. Three engines are offered: the 1407cc OHV unit shared with the Corolla 1400, with a crossflow cylinder head and an output of 86ps; a 1588cc version created by enlarging the bore, producing 100ps; and a twin-carburetor version of the latter developing 105ps. There is also a wide choice of transmissions: a 3-speed column shift, a 4-speed floor shift, and, on the 1600cc models, even a 5-speed. This is one of the Carina’s major attractions. The suspension is unlike that of any previous model. At the front is a conventional MacPherson-strut arrangement, while the rear uses a layout basically similar to that of the Crown, with a rigid axle located by four links and a Panhard rod and supported by coil springs. Both two- and four-door bodies are available, with prices ranging from 509,000 yen for the 1400 Standard with 3-speed column shift to 720,000 yen for the four-door 1600ST with twin carburetors and 5-speed.

Toyota Carina 1600GT (1971)

Publication: Car Graphic
Format: Road Test
Date: July 1971
Author: “C/G Test Group” (uncredited)

Summary: A family sports car combining the two-door Carina ST body with a 1.6-liter DOHC engine. Excellent 5-speed gearbox. The engine is flexible enough to use fifth gear from 60km/h, making it well-suited even to low-speed driving in the city. Impressive fade resistance from disc/drum brakes. Handling is at a high standard even on cross-ply tires. Well-balanced engine and chassis. Good passenger accommodation and interior equipment. Fuel economy is rather poor.
